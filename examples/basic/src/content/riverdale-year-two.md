## Background

The first year was about proving the concept: a branded presence on every phone, managed by people who already run programs — not a dev shop on retainer.

By month six, the question shifted from *“Will anyone install this?”* to *“What else should we put here?”* Volunteers asked for push reminders before classes. Donors wanted quick links to give again. Families bookmarked program calendars they actually checked.

## What changed in year two

Staff stopped treating the app as a brochure. It became the default place to publish schedule changes, volunteer shifts, and seasonal campaigns — the same content that used to die in email threads.

The center also rewrote onboarding copy after listening to first-time users. Shorter paths to “sign up for a class” and clearer language around who qualifies for Gap Ministries resources.

## Editing this page

Frontmatter fields and body headings/paragraphs are tagged for the inline editor — open **Inline Editor** in the Astro toolbar and tweak them on the rendered page.

You can also paste new `##` sections directly into this markdown file, save, and refresh.

## Why Markdown fits some stories

JSON works when every field has a fixed slot in a complex layout. Markdown fits when the story is mostly prose and you want to add or reorder sections without reshaping a schema.

Both compile to static HTML at build time — no production performance difference.
